# ClickOps Sentinel brand and design system

One design language for everything ClickOps Sentinel shows people: this
repository's README, the alert emails, the Slack and Teams messages, and a
future landing page. If you change how an alert looks, start here.

## The idea

A meerkat colony posts a sentinel. While the others dig and forage, one
meerkat stands upright on a mound and watches the horizon, and calls out the
moment something approaches.

ClickOps Sentinel does that job for an AWS account. Your team works through
infrastructure as code; the sentinel watches the console (and the API, for AI
agents) and calls out the manual click the moment it lands, with enough
context to decide what to do.

The mascot is **Scout**, a meerkat standing guard. In the logo Scout looks up
at an amber click ripple with a pointer: the manual change it just spotted.

| Element | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scout, upright on a mound | Always on watch, never in the way |
| Amber click ripple | The manual change: what Sentinel exists to catch |
| Night background | Calm, quiet by default: no alert, no noise |

## Name and voice

- Product name: **ClickOps Sentinel**. Write it in full on first use; after
  that "Sentinel" is fine. Never "Clickops", "Click-Ops", or "CS".
- Tagline: **Every click, watched.**
- Promise: **Manual changes to your AWS accounts, caught and explained in
  seconds.**
- Voice: a calm colleague on watch. State what happened, why it matters, and
  what to do next. No alarmism, no blame: the person who clicked usually had
  a reason, and the alert should help find it.

Writing rules, enforced by tests for rendered alerts (`_assert_style` in
`tests/test_email_render.py`):

- No em-dashes or en-dashes. Use a comma, a colon, or two sentences.
- No emojis, in alerts, docs, or commit messages.
- Lead with the outcome. The title says what happened; details follow.
- Plain words over jargon. "Opened SSH to the internet", not "ingress
  0.0.0.0/0 delta detected".
- Identifiers (event names, IPs, ARNs, times) appear verbatim, in monospace.

## Logo files

All files live in this folder and are generated by `make brand`
(`scripts/render_brand.py`) from one mascot definition, so the variants
never drift. Edit the script, not the SVGs.

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `logo-mark.svg`, `logo-mark-512.png` | Primary mark: avatars, the README, app icons |
| `logo-icon.svg`, `logo-icon-64.png`, `favicon-32.png` | Head-and-shoulders crop for 64 px and below |
| `logo-horizontal-light.svg` | Mark plus wordmark on light backgrounds |
| `logo-horizontal-dark.svg` | Mark plus wordmark on dark backgrounds |
| `mascot.svg` | Scout without the badge, for illustrations and the landing page |
| `social-card.svg`, `social-card.png` | 1280 x 640 GitHub social preview and link unfurls |

Usage:

- Keep the mark on its night badge. Do not recolor Scout or the ripple.
- Minimum size: 48 px for `logo-mark`, below that use `logo-icon`.
- Clear space around the mark: at least a quarter of its width.
- The wordmark is "ClickOps" in white (dark backgrounds) or night (light
  backgrounds), and "Sentinel" in signal amber.

## Color tokens

The single source of truth is [`tokens.json`](tokens.json). The email
renderer carries the same values (`COLOR` and `FONT` in `email_render.py`)
and `test_design_tokens_match_brand_file` fails if they diverge.

Brand and neutrals:

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `night` | `#0f1420` | Email background and header, logo badge, primary button |
| `night_raised` | `#1e293b` | Secondary pills on night, the mound under Scout |
| `ink` | `#1e293b` | Body text |
| `slate` | `#64748b` | Section labels, secondary text |
| `mist` | `#94a3b8` | Timestamps, footer, neutral stripe |
| `line` | `#e2e8f0` | Dividers |
| `surface` | `#f1f5f9` | Neutral fact tiles |
| `paper` | `#f8fafc` | Footer and neutral callouts |
| `signal` | `#f59e0b` | Brand accent: the click, "this alert" in the session path, "Sentinel" in the wordmark |
| `sky` | `#7dd3fc` | Reserved for links and informational accents on night |

Status colors, each with a `_soft` background and an `_ink` text color:

| Family | Base | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `danger` | `#ef4444` | High or critical, suspicious, sign-in alerts |
| `signal` | `#f59e0b` | Manual change confirmed, medium, machine-driven changes |
| `safe` | `#22c55e` | Low or none, likely sanctioned, savings |
| `info` | `#3b82f6` | Recommendations |

Severity always maps the same way across the email cards, the criticality
catalog block, the session path, and the status stripe: none and low are
`safe`, medium is `signal` (with `signal_tint`), high and critical are
`danger`.

Mascot colors (`fur`, `shade`, `cream`, `umber`) are for artwork only, never
for interface elements.

## Typography

- Sans: `-apple-system, "Segoe UI", Roboto, Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`.
  System fonts load instantly in every mail client and look native.
- Mono: `SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas, "Liberation Mono", monospace`, for
  identifiers only: event names in the session path, IPs, timestamps,
  account and region, Stratus technique IDs.
- Scale used in emails: 20 px bold title, 14 px body, 12 px secondary, 10 px
  bold uppercase section labels with 0.5 px letter spacing.

## Email anatomy

Every email is built from the same components in `email_render.py`, in the
same order. Leave a block out when it has nothing to say; never reorder.

1. **Status stripe** (`_header`, `TONES`): 4 px bar across the top of the card.
   Red for high, critical, suspicious, and sign-in alerts; green for likely
   sanctioned work; amber otherwise; grey for operational notices.
2. **Header**: the text wordmark (amber signal dot, CLICKOPS SENTINEL),
   account and region on the right in mono, the title, then pills (verdict,
   confidence, follow-up, level).
3. **Who block** (`_who_block`): actor in bold, IP and time in mono, source
   network, client, session anomalies, then "Driven by" with up to four
   evidence lines and any "Signals disagree" warning.
4. **Fact tiles** (`_cards`): three tiles. Investigated alerts show Action,
   Security, Cost impact. Plain alerts and follow-ups show Action,
   Criticality, Result (`_facts_row`). Sign-in alerts show Outcome, MFA,
   Region.
5. **Criticality catalog** (`_criticality_block`): rule, tactic, ATT&CK and
   Stratus references, why it matched.
6. **Narrative sections** (`_section`): Purpose, Session, Security, Cost,
   History, each under a section label (`_eyebrow`).
7. **Session path** (`_session_path`): the vertical stepper of the session,
   with the triggering step in signal amber and marked "this alert".
8. **Callout** (`_callout`): Recommendation (info), Why no new investigation
   (neutral), or an operational notice (neutral).
9. **Button** (`_cta`): "View event in CloudTrail". Raw URLs never appear in
   the body.
10. **Footer**: product name, tagline, license link.

The five email types use this anatomy:

| Email | Function | Stripe |
|---|---|---|
| Investigated change | `enriched_email` | By verdict and severity |
| Plain change (AI off or unavailable) | `plain_email` | By criticality |
| Same session, additional change | `followup_email` | By criticality |
| Console sign-in alert | `login_email` | Red |
| Operational notice | `note_email` | Grey |

Email HTML rules: table layout, inline styles, no images, no external
resources, no scripts. The brand comes through the wordmark and colors, so
an email looks the same with images blocked and never phones home.
`test_emails_carry_no_images_or_external_resources` enforces this.

## Chat messages

Slack and Microsoft Teams messages go through Amazon Q Developer, which
renders markdown only, so the design system there is structure and wording:

- Title: the same text as the email subject, including the `[HIGH]` or
  `[CRITICAL]` prefix for those levels.
- Body: a lead sentence when the title needs one, then `*Label*: value`
  fields in a stable order, ending with `<link|View event in CloudTrail>`.
- Field labels match the email: Criticality, Who, Action, Client, Driven by,
  Evidence, Signals disagree, Result.
- Follow-ups thread under the first alert of the session.

## Landing page

Not built yet. When it is, reuse this system rather than inventing a new one:

- Night hero with `mascot.svg` on the right, the wordmark and tagline on the
  left (the social card is the reference layout).
- Real email screenshots from `make screenshots`, never mockups that drift
  from the templates.
- `tokens.json` drives the CSS custom properties, for example
  `--color-signal: #f59e0b`.

## Regenerating assets

```bash
make brand          # logo, icon, mascot, lockups, social card (SVG + PNG)
make screenshots    # README email and Slack screenshots, from the real templates
make preview-email  # all five email types in docs/email-preview.html
```

`make brand` and `make screenshots` need Google Chrome or Chromium for
headless rendering; `make screenshots` also needs ImageMagick to trim the
captures.
